# Dialogue performance notes

## First-year Sky
- He is not a child. He is a first-year college student, only about two years younger.
- Early delivery: curious, excitable, slightly impatient for results.
- Middle delivery: surprised by the speed of the first build, then confused by the pivots and lack of customers.
- Final delivery: softer and vulnerable; he is asking whether the dream was foolish.

## Third-year Sky
- Same youthful vocal identity; slightly steadier and more measured.
- Do not make him sound bitter or rude. He is direct because he already knows the outcome.
- The most important pauses are after “I don't know how to write the ending yet”, “Zero replies” and “Zero.”
- At the emotional turn, he physically leans forward and looks at First-year Sky rather than looking away.

## Naturalism rules
- Let contractions remain: “we'd”, “didn't”, “I'd”.
- Keep the exact words in the script, but allow natural breaths and slight timing variation within each time window.
- Don't add forced Bengaluru slang. Use the speaker's real accent and rhythm. A natural “bro” can be tried during the voice recording only if it sounds like Sky; do not insert slang just to signal location.
- No motivational speech delivery. The last minute should feel intimate, not inspirational advertising.
