# TREATMENT — DID WE MAKE IT?

180 s · 1920×1080 · 24 fps · style `cel-anime-80s`, restrained (PLAN §3) · six 30 s clips on one timeline.
Story, dialogue and timecodes are the ones in `SCRIPT_TIMELINE.md` / `dialogue.json`; nothing here rewrites them.
This document decides how the film is shot, lit, performed, cut and scored. Shot-by-shot detail is in `SHOT_LIST.md`.
Written before opening the style's `DEMO.md`.

---

## 1. Core idea and emotional arc

**Core idea.** A young man asks his own self two years on the question he's been carrying since day one, and the
answer is honest: not yet. The film shouldn't say "I made it." It should say: *I haven't made it yet, but now I
understand the next step better.* (PLAN, final test.)

**Logline.** Two years after arriving in Bengaluru to build a company, First-year Sky asks Third-year Sky "Did we make
it?", and gets an honest account instead of a success story: three product attempts, one reply, fifty silences, a
pause, and a new reason to go outside.

**Chosen structure** (from three candidates, DIRECTOR §4):
1. *The locked two-hander that opens into memories* (scripted; **chosen**).
2. A single journey told backwards from the door to the first-year bus (rejected: it hides the honest answer behind a
   trick and loses the asks/answers rhythm).
3. One room, four colour models, memories told only by objects on the desk (rejected on its own: Bengaluru and the
   campus vanish). **Its best idea is kept:** the conversation room is one painted place whose light changes across
   the film, so the room itself carries the arc.

**Emotional arc** (PLAN §1), and where each step lives:

| Step | Time | First-year Sky | Third-year Sky |
|---|---|---|---|
| Curiosity | 0–30 | expects a success story | guarded, hands him a blank page |
| Excitement | 60–94 | the four-hour build, the one reply | matter-of-fact, a little dry |
| Confusion | 94–120 | three directions, no customers yet | factual, not ashamed |
| Disappointment | 120–164 | fifty emails, zero replies, zero money | direct, no self-pity |
| Empathy | 164–176 | "Was I wrong to dream that big?" | leans forward for the first time, warm |
| Forward motion | 176–180 | "Let's go." | follows him out |

## 2. Opening hook

Half a second of black, then a wide, still two-shot: **two young men who are obviously the same person** sit across
one desk, a single blank sheet between them, one lamp, a fan turning. No music. The first sound after the room is
a question: "So… did we make it?" The hook is the image itself (same face, two years apart) plus the question every
viewer asks of their own past. No title, no logo reveal before it.

## 3. Ending

Morning has come into the same room. Third-year Sky says he's exploring physical AI (a pencil sketch of a sensor
system on the paper, plainly unfinished). Both stand. First-year Sky reaches the door first, Third-year follows, and
the door opens on daylight and outdoor sound. "Let's go." The end card `DID WE MAKE IT?` / `NOT YET. STILL BUILDING.`
fades up from 176 s over the walk and holds to 180 s.

**The echo:** the closing wide repaints the opening wide: same desk, same sheet, the night lamp now beaten by
morning, the chairs left behind. It should feel like a door opening, not a victory.

## 4. Scene transitions

One grammar for the whole film: **the paper and the screens carry us between worlds.** No digital dissolves or wipes
(STYLE §6); cuts, graphic match cuts, push-ins through a surface, and one restrained white bloom.

| Seam | Transition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 29.5–30.0 (Scene 1 → 2) | push into the blank sheet until its white fills the frame → **match cut** to the bright bus window | the signature move: the unwritten page becomes the past |
| 60.0 (Scene 2 → 3) | graphic match: the closed notebook → the laptop lid/screen in the same frame position | the dream becomes the work |
| 72.0 | cut on the downbeat into the build montage | the film's acceleration |
| 90.0 (Scene 3 → 4) | cut from the email insert to the room, the same notification still on the laptop | the memory's result sits on the present desk |
| 120.0 (Scene 4 → 5) | **hard cut with the music stopping** | the turn: from what he built to who used it |
| 138.0 | truck-in through the laptop screen into the outreach montage | entering the online world |
| 145.5 | pull back out of the screen to the two-shot | back to the room for the lesson |
| 150.0 (Scene 5 → 6) | cut; the window starts to carry the first morning light | the room's colour model turns |
| 171–175 | memory images laid over the room on the paper, then gone | the pause and what came out of it |
| 179–180 | the door opens; daylight spills over the frame | the bookend |

Sound transitions: the bus engine starts under the page at ~26 s (J-cut), and the morning air runs on under the end
card (L-cut).

## 6. Camera grammar

**Room geography, fixed for the film:** First-year Sky screen **left** facing right; Third-year Sky screen **right**
facing left; the 180° line runs through both heads. Lamp at Third-year's end of the desk; window behind the desk;
**door screen left**, behind First-year.

| Move | Used for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Locked two-shot | conversation, the baseline | 0.5, 145.5, 177 s |
| Slow push-in | a question or admission tightening | 0.5–3, 20–25, 169–176 s |
| Push into a surface | entering memory | 25–30 (paper), 138 (screen) |
| Over-shoulder, always from the line's same side | exchange of an object or a look | 7–10, 133–138 s |
| Side tracking, three parallax layers | steady momentum of arrival | 33–41 s |
| Small figure, huge painted plate | ambition against a big city | 41–47 s |
| Crane up/down a drawing | the dream growing and folding back | 47–55 s |
| Overhead locked | a year of ideas and no product | 60–72 s |
| Quick cut montage on bars | the four-hour build | 72–82 s |
| Held close-up | admissions: "the ending", "zero replies", "Zero." | 20–25, 126–133, 156–158 s |
| Inserts with a sliding highlight | objects that matter: paper, emails, cards, the target | throughout |

Rules: camera and light move on ones; characters on twos. Every move has a reason (`SHOT_LIST.md` gives it). Subjects
fill at least a third of the frame height at key moments; the lower ~120 px stay clear for captions.

## 7. Sound design

Three layers: ambience, foley tied to every action, and a restrained original score. Two real silences: after "Zero
replies" (~129 s) and after "Zero." (~157 s).

**Score:** original, 96 bpm, so a bar = 2.5 s and each 30 s clip is exactly 12 bars. Every seam lands on a downbeat.
The palette is the style's city-pop instruments used quietly: FM electric piano with a bell transient, warm synth
pad, soft drum machine, light slap bass, 16th guitar chanks low in the mix, a soft brass-like synth line only at the
end. No gated-snare anthem, no generic piano and strings, no copied song.

| Time | Ambience | Foley | Music |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 night room | hostel fan, distant traffic | chair creak, paper slide, paper turn, breath, waveform shimmer | **none** |
| 30–60 memory | bus engine (from ~26 s), street wash, distant horns; corridor | bus brakes, footsteps, bag strap, pencil | the pulse begins on the cut |
| 60–72 thinking | still room, laptop fan | keyboard taps stop at 60 | pad only |
| 72–90 first build | laptop fan | typing and terminal ticks on the cuts, deploy click, checkout click, 10 send whooshes, **one** notification | rhythm picks up |
| 90–120 three directions | room | paper unfolding, card placements, keyboard rhythm | shifts down on "wrong customers", rises, **hard stop at 120** |
| 120–150 shipping ≠ selling | cool, still room tone | scrolls, notification blips, laptop turned on the desk | near silence; true silence on "Zero" |
| 150–176 morning comes | early outdoor air through the window | calendar pages, book shift, breath | e-piano alone, wide; a gentle lift from 169 |
| 176–180 door | outdoor morning floods in | chairs pushed back, footsteps, door latch | resolves on the open door |

Voice ~10 dB above ducked music. Master −14 LUFS (`mux.sh`).

## 8. Colour progression

The agreed palette (PLAN §6): deep ink `#111820`, midnight blue `#1D2B3A`, slate `#40576A`, warm ivory `#F1EBDD`,
muted amber `#D3A15F`, muted teal `#6F9B9A`, soft sage `#8B9A88`, rust `#A76550` (friction only). Each lighting state is
its **own colour model** for plates and cels (skin has its own colour in every model), never a filter over the frame.

| Time | State | Dominant | Accent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–30 | night conversation room | midnight, ink | lamp amber, paper ivory |
| 30–60 | first-year memory, late afternoon | warm amber, concrete grey, faded campus green | muted blue sky |
| 60–90 | build nights (memory desk) | deep navy | monitor teal, lamp amber |
| 90–120 | room, building | navy, slate | teal; rust only at the "wrong customers" split |
| 120–150 | no replies | desaturated slate, cool teal | none; stillness |

Restraint: CRT/tape texture reduced to a hint (fine scanlines, faint grille, light chroma bleed, low noise); bloom only
on the lamp, monitor and door; no neon, no chrome titles, no rain.

## 9. Character performance

(Full detail in `CHARACTER_BIBLE.md`.)

- **Same person, two years apart.** Distinguished by clothes, posture, energy and delivery; never by age.
- **First-year Sky:** sits forward, reacts quickly, gestures more, eyes brighter; confidence mixed with shyness. He
  changes the most: hopeful → excited → confused → worried → quietly determined.
- **Third-year Sky:** contained, sits back, elbows near the desk; often looks at the page before a hard truth; a
  little dry, never cruel or bitter. The one big physical change in the film: **he leans forward into the lamp at
  169 s**, the first time he crosses toward his younger self.
- **Limited animation, careful acting.** Characters on twos; blinks, lip flaps and gestures on threes; every gesture
  has anticipation and follow-through; holds are performance, not freezes (breath and hair cycles keep moving).
- **Naturalism:** lines are delivered as written, with breaths and small imperfect pauses; no trailer or
  motivational delivery; no slang added; no exaggerated anime faces (no sweat drops, giant tears or chibi).
- Mouths move only on on-camera lines; V.O. lines (T03, T04, T05, T06, T07, T11) keep mouths closed.
