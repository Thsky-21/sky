# DID WE MAKE IT? — Script v2
4 Nov 2026 · replaces SCRIPT_TIMELINE.md and SHOT_LIST.md

- **Words:** `dialogue.json` (v2) is the source of truth. **Direction and picture:** this file.
- **Runtime:** about 2:20–2:40. The real length depends on the clone's pace; the hard cap is 3:00. Times in the hook map are approximate.
- **Set:** one set, the existing room. There are no memory locations. Memories are drawn on the sheet of paper.

---

## The idea in one line
First-year Sky asks "Did we make it?" Third-year Sky hands him a blank page. The younger one says: **"Then don't write it. Say it."** Everything after that is said, not written. In the last shot, the page gets its first line, spoken into a phone.

## Why this version holds attention
- **Hook:** the film opens on a glowing phone playing a messy voice note in a dark room. By 0:07 the viewer has the question they want answered.
- **Engine:** the younger self keeps expecting a success story, and the older self keeps puncturing it. The laughs come first, so the hits land harder later.
- **Turn:** the younger one asks if he was wrong to dream big. The older one says no. Then the younger one gives something back: "You're still talking to me."
- **Voice is the theme, not a sticker:** he can't write his story; he can only say it. The ending repeats the opening voice note, minus the "um". No logo is needed.

## Characters
- **FY — First-year Sky** (screen left). Leans forward. Fast, bright, impatient, a little shy. Asks everything.
- **TY — Third-year Sky** (screen right). Leans back. Dry and slower; says less than he knows. He leans into the lamp only once, from T17 onward.
- Same face and same age band. The difference comes from posture, pace and clothes. They never hold mirror poses.

## Sound rules
- Room tone (fan, distant traffic) runs under everything.
- No music until T17 ("No."). From there, one original sparse piano motif that resolves on the end card.
- Two near-silences: after T11 ("Zero replies") and after T13 ("Zero.").
- VN01 sounds like it's playing from a phone speaker.

## What changed from v1, and why
1. **Logic fix.** In v1, First-year Sky says "You used to send me voice notes", but his future self can't have sent him voice notes. Now voice notes are their shared habit.
2. **The résumé is gone.** The tech list (TypeScript, Python, React, Redis) and the product explanations are now plain words plus paper drawings. Reviewers feel a story; they skim a résumé.
3. **Four memory locations became one sheet of paper.** The bus, corridor, campus and 2024 desk are replaced by drawings on the sheet. Same information, one set, buildable tonight.
4. **Questions became a pattern.** Interview questions ("What finally got us moving?") are replaced by a rhythm: he hopes, gets punctured, hopes again. That rhythm is what keeps people watching.
5. **The door exit is gone.** Two walk cycles are expensive and generic. The phone voice note replaces them and pays off both the opening and the blank page.
6. **Timing comes from your takes.** Lines are no longer squeezed into fixed timecode windows.

All facts come from your raw story: four-to-five-hour build, domain within days, 10 emails / 1 reply, wrong customers, spending limits, five months on agent safety, up till 3 am, 50 emails / 0 replies, the removed Reddit post, zero revenue, the $5K target, placements in July, never all in, the two-month stop, AI ideas as a trap, physical AI as exploration.

## Hook map: why nobody skips
| Time | What holds them |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | A glowing phone in a dark room playing a messy voice note. Unusual sound and image; who is this, and what's the idea? |
| 0:05–0:07 | "So… did we make it?" between two identical guys. The whole premise in one line. |
| 0:08–0:14 | The blank-page gag (flip, flip, hold it to the lamp). First smile. |
| 0:14–0:22 | "What happened to that guy?" / "I don't know how it ends yet." Opens a loop that only closes in the last shot. |
| 0:22–0:27 | "Then don't write it. Say it." plus the title. The rule of the film. |
| 0:27–1:15 | Hope, puncture, hope. A laugh every 10–15 s: "Calm down", "One reply!", "You sleep at twelve, right?", "Of course they did." A new paper drawing every few seconds. |
| 1:15–1:30 | "Money?" "Zero." Silence. The first real punch. |
| 1:30–2:00 | "Was I wrong?" "No." The music arrives for the first time. Then the reversal: "You're still talking to me." |
| 2:00–2:15 | Payoff: the same voice note, now without the "um", writes itself onto the blank page. "NOT YET." |

Rules that protect the curve:
- **No shot longer than about 6 s** except the two held moments (T11 and Y19).
- **Never two paper inserts in a row for more than 12 s.** Cut back to a face.
- **Radio test before animating:** play the generated lines in order with no picture. If the audio alone doesn't hold you for 2 minutes, fix the voice before touching the animation.

## Cloned-voice generation sheet
**Same clone, two performances.** Get the difference from settings and speed, not pitch. Shift pitch by 1 semitone at most, or it starts to sound fake.

| | First-year (FY) | Third-year (TY) |
|---|---|---|
| Expressiveness / variability | Higher (looser, more emotion) | Lower (steadier) |
| Speed | About 1.05× | About 0.92–0.95× |
| Feel | Leans in, quick | Holds back, dry |

**Generating the lines**
- **Beats are split into parts.** Clones rush pauses. Lines with a beat have a `parts` list in `dialogue.json`. Generate each part separately (`T11_p1`, `T11_p2`); Claude Code joins them with the exact `beat_s` of silence.
- **Punctuation drives emotion.** Keep the ellipses (…), dashes (—) and the "!" exactly as written. Y12 "Did you try—" must end cut off.
- **Three variants of the key lines:** VN01, Y01, T02, T04, T11, T13, Y15, T17, Y19 and T22. Pick by ear and keep the one that sounds least like reading.
- **Breaths:** if your tool supports breath or non-speech sounds, allow them on FY's lines and T17.
- **Naming:** `voices/<ID>.wav`, or `voices/<ID>_p1.wav` / `_p2.wav` for split lines.

**VN01 is the one line clones are worst at.** Real stumbling ("Um… no, wait") is the whole effect. Optional: record only VN01 raw on your phone (30 seconds). Messy real voice at the start and a clean clone at the end (T22) actually fits the film's idea. Your call.

**Before generating:** read the script aloud once and change any word that isn't you, in both files.
